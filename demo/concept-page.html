Journey Island

A living world where each completed step brings a new region to life. The island starts grey. Finish a step and your traveller walks the road, the next region floods with colour, trees and buildings grow, and villagers, sheep, boats and clouds start moving. Drag to look around. Try night mode too.

Journey complete!

Sample 7-day Bible reading plan. Built with Three.js (free, MIT) for the 3D world and GSAP (free) for the timing. Every model here is made in code from simple shapes. In a real app you'd use free, ready-made art from Kenney or Quaternius, which looks far richer.

Progress changes the world

Each step unlocks a region. Colour spreads out from its centre and the scenery grows in a wave. People see their progress as a place, not a percentage.

The world keeps moving

Villagers wander, sheep graze, the windmill turns, a boat circles the island and cloud shadows drift over the ground. That's what makes it feel like a simulation game.

Rewards you can see

XP pop-ups, sparkle bursts, a golden road behind you, and fireworks at the end. At night, windows and the lighthouse light up in regions you've unlocked.

Engines that can build a world like this

All free, including for commercial use. Pick a 3D engine for an island or city like the one above, or a 2D engine for a classic level-select map like Mario World or Candy Crush.

Three.js + React Three Fiber

Top pick · 3D worldnpm i three @react-three/fiber @react-three/drei

What the demo above uses. Three.js is the most widely used 3D library on the web. React Three Fiber lets you build the scene as React components, and its helper library (drei) adds ready-made cameras, model loading, sky, clouds, water and soft shadows.

What I'd build

  1. React Three Fiber + drei for the world, with Kenney or Quaternius models instead of shapes made in code. That gets you this demo, but beautiful.
  2. One region per milestone in your roadmap. Completing it in the app triggers the unlock animation the next time they open the map.
  3. Keep a simple list view next to it, for older phones and for people using screen readers.
  4. If you'd rather have a flat illustrated map (cheaper to make, lighter on phones), use Phaser 4 or PixiJS with a Kenney tileset instead.
